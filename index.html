Story

I live in Lecco. The lake is at the bottom and the rock starts right behind town.

I spend as much time as I can up there. Skis on my back, or a rope, or a bike with too many bags.

It started with a spark, though. My grandfather, a boat battery and a wrench. I was ten and fascinated.

You notice things in the mountains. Snow comes later. Glaciers pull back between visits. Old photos look like other places.

I can't fix that with a wrench. But I do know electricity.

So I design microgrids. Solar, batteries, and a lot of Python. The goal is simple: burn less of what shouldn't be burned.

The mountains also taught me how to plan. You check the forecast, then assume it's wrong, and you leave yourself a margin.

Load forecasting is the same game. The forecast is never right. Good dispatch is deciding well anyway.

Same habits, different terrain. Mostly I'm just trying to stay curious and keep track of my earbuds.

Work

muGrid Analytics, Golden CO. Senior Engineer. Microgrid design, load forecasting and dispatch optimization, for remote towns, campuses and everything between.

Before that: solar trackers at Solaflect in Vermont, hydro turbines in Rwanda, and a PhD at Politecnico di Milano on forecasting and energy management. Dartmouth engineering before all of it. The resume has the details.

Tools: Python (pandas, TensorFlow, Pyomo), System Advisor Model, MATLAB, HOMER, QGIS, a soldering iron. English native, Italian professional.
